What is a threat? (and how is it different from a vulnerability)

Think of the hospital system as a house.

Threat
Someone who wants to cause harm, and what they want to do. A thief who wants to steal your things is a threat. A threat needs a threat actor: a person or group with a reason to attack.
Vulnerability
A weakness that lets the threat succeed. An unlocked door or an open window is a vulnerability. A vulnerability on its own does no harm. It becomes a problem when a threat uses it.
Asset
Something valuable that needs protecting, like the things inside the house. In the hospital, the patient records in the databases are the most important assets.
Trust boundary
The line where data moves between areas that trust each other differently, like the front door between the street and your home. Trust boundaries are where attacks usually happen. You can see them as dashed boxes in the DFD.
What does STRIDE mean?

STRIDE is a checklist of six kinds of threats. Pick the one that best describes what the attacker wants to do, and write its name in the STRIDE column.

Category What it means Example in the hospital
Spoofing Pretending to be someone else. An attacker logs in as a doctor with a stolen password.
Tampering Changing data that you should not be able to change. Someone changes a patient's medication in the records.
Repudiation Doing something and denying it, because nothing proves who did it. A booking is deleted and the logs cannot show who did it.
Information disclosure Seeing data that you should not be able to see. Patient records leak to people outside the hospital.
Denial of service Making the system stop working for everyone else. The booking site is flooded so patients cannot book.
Elevation of privilege Getting more access than you should have. A normal user gets admin rights in the backend.
